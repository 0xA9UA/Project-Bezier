The consolidated view

Working mechanisms, real storage gains, and one promising steering pilot

The portfolio now spans original sphere models, shared fields, compression and runtime studies, coding agents, four nested-core revisions, and eight pretrained 7B revisions. Its strongest repeatable lessons concern what geometry changes, where storage or memory can be reduced, and which simple controls explain the gains. Useful learned steering has a positive single-seed result in nested-core v4, while broader language-model and coding advantages remain unestablished.

Mechanism

Routes change computation; changed computation is not automatically better

The original sphere learned task-dependent expert paths, and activation interventions in later routing studies changed subsequent group or neuron selections. V6 of the pretrained 7B line went further: coordinates selected actual MLP nodes, with coordinate changes altering logits. These are substantive implementation results. Across the original scaling, routing and steering studies, however, simpler straight, fixed, ordinary-routing or compact residual controls usually matched or exceeded measured quality or efficiency.

Storage

Compact representations work when their assumptions fit the task

Shared fields reduced storage on structured handwriting and known-association tasks, but larger recall sets and corruptions exposed capacity and robustness tradeoffs. Post-training compression produced practical artifacts: Granite's six-bit model passed limited quality screens at 22.245 GiB, and a later validation-selected mixed-precision artifact passed its fresh screen at 16.464 GiB. Uniform six-bit, fixed four-bit and random mixed controls also remain important evidence. The gains come from sharing, quantization and compact replacements; they do not establish exceptional knowledge density from curvature alone.

Runtime

Measure complete execution; fewer operations or less VRAM can cost more time

CPU/GPU splits and sparse routes were functionally correct, but dependent transfers and dispatch frequently outweighed arithmetic savings. The small CPU-actor/GPU-learner PPO experiment is a positive exception. Offloading in pretrained 7B v3 greatly reduced GPU allocation while sharply increasing latency. V7 substantially improved the earlier route executor, yet learned routes still cost more than its fixed and dense controls. Storage, peak tensors, actual latency and parameter-application counts should remain separate measures.

From scratch

Bounded internal memory did not establish a working coding agent

The small and 7.35B MemorySphere projects demonstrated custom full-stack training, fixed scratch-state memory and checkpoint integrity. Their task results remained weak. The later 7.41B ten-hour run finished 22,042 updates and 4.77 million supervised tokens, improving token losses but producing no successful coding workflows. Final scoring was time-limited: 0/60 tasks at eight visits and 0/40 with adaptive stopping, from two requested 96-task panels. The later check-in found invalid tool actions throughout those recorded panels.

Positive pilot

Preserving input information made steering useful in nested-core v4

The nested-core sequence progressed from shared-slice feasibility to a fixed whole core, larger cores, and an input-preserving skip. In v4's matched seed-7 experiment, enabling redirects increased the same shared checkpoint's held-out accuracy from 52.73% to 57.62%; NLL gains versus zero turns, one turn and the separately trained clean model had positive paired-program intervals. Both clean and shared models benefited from the representation change. This is a meaningful local result, still limited by one seed, reused holdouts, near-chance longer-program core reasoning and higher latency than clean inference.

Pretrained 7B

Learned 7B control still needs to beat fixed and shuffled routes

The pretrained Mistral line verified exact shared storage, bounded update scope and progressively richer routing. V7's stage policy did not outperform fixed or shuffled controls; its arbitrary recurrent pilot lost answer quality, and the latest RL continuation remained at 50% with a constant answer and path after 512 total updates. V8 produced diverse actions and a small test-point advantage, but intervals and shuffled controls did not establish input-specific benefit. Its frozen release rule therefore keeps fixed (2,2) as the application default. V8 branches from stage-ordered v7, not its later RL continuation.

Coding

Extra visits and controller RL have not yielded dependable coding gains

Think's recurrent small model did not approach its 14B capability reference; Revisit's compute-matched routing comparison did not beat ordinary recurrence. LongHorizon's compact pre-RL variant solved one additional task in its four-task pilot, but the difference depended on an output cap, and controller RL regressed both that result and cumulative workflow behavior. These studies support preserving trained zero-step, ordinary recurrence and pre-RL controls, while scoring complete tool workflows rather than attractive routes or isolated token-loss improvements.

The archive is rich, but its experiments are not one combined benchmark

Saved protocols, per-example outcomes, audits and source/checkpoint identities make the work reviewable. Comparisons still span different tasks, denominators, seeds, model sizes, training budgets, precision formats and execution paths. Priority next steps
  • Replicate nested-core v4 with additional seeds and a newly sequestered program holdout. Keep input-preserving clean/shared arms, zero-turn and one-turn controls, and record later-decision utility as well as total accuracy.
  • For pretrained 7B routing, require gains over validation-selected fixed and shuffled policies before enabling learned control by default. Treat stage routing, arbitrary recurrence and RL continuation as distinct experiments.
  • For from-scratch coding, diagnose valid tool generation and cached/full-context behavior before extending training. Complete the fixed held-out panels and report code correctness, valid edits and finished workflows separately from token loss.
  • Choose a practical storage or runtime target, then compare complete artifacts and full request cost under matched quality and runtime conditions. Preserve simpler quantizers, compact residuals, CPU-only execution and dense/fixed routes as controls.

Original sphere and scaling studies

bezier_sphere

Test whether input-dependent cubic Bezier routes through a sphere can select useful expert computation, then measure accuracy, storage, speed and robustness against simpler controls.

Saved status Completed local proof of concept, trained scaling study, untrained execution sweep and algebra checks; no overall geometry advantage established.
Work completed or attempted
  • Implemented learned surface entry/exit, interior control points, sparse top-k expert dispatch, a carried feature vector and a trained decoder; added a scalar Bezier-transfer variant.
  • Trained five digit-classification architectures across three seeds on a fixed 1,078/359/360 split; saved checkpoints, route interventions, CPU/GPU verification and an offline viewer.
  • Completed 92 scaling runs using EMNIST, up to 100,000 training images, and synthetic association recall up to 16,384 facts; compared routed, full-residual and compact-residual models.
  • Benchmarked 15 untrained execution configurations up to 68.1 million parameters; derived and numerically checked routing factorization, structured weight sharing and restricted commuting dynamics.
  • Published architecture, reproduction and methodology documents plus shared activation and recurrent-computation visualizations.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    Original digits: Bezier routing achieved 96.76% mean accuracy across seeds 7, 19 and 42, versus 96.67% for residual and 97.13% for straight routing. Bezier batch-one latency was 0.992 ms versus 0.471 ms residual; no general accuracy or speed advantage was established. S003 S017

  2. reported

    Routes affect computation: shuffling routes lowered mean digit accuracy from 96.76% to 88.52%, bypassing interior transformations to 64.91%, and straightening trained curves to 96.30%. These are interventions on trained models, not retrained controls. S001 S017

  3. measured

    At the largest trained EMNIST setting, Bezier used 8.900 MiB at 83.59% accuracy and 1.234 ms batch-one latency; compact residual used 2.830 MiB at 84.04% and 0.457 ms. Means cover two seeds and 18,800 test images. S005 S006

  4. measured

    At 16,384 synthetic associations, medium Bezier reached 99.98% fresh-query recall in 1.130 MiB; medium compact residual reached 100% in 0.605 MiB. Stronger-noise recall was 69.43% versus 61.06%, showing a task-specific tradeoff rather than a universal storage/reliability win. S005 S006

  5. measured

    The saved verification covers all 92 scaling checkpoints and passes source, data, partition, checkpoint and report-link checks. The original digit verification separately reloads all 15 checkpoints. These are recorded checks, not tests rerun for this index. S007 S004

  6. measured

    At 68.1M random-weight parameters, Bezier versus full residual latency was 0.969 versus 3.012 ms at batch one, but 5.767 versus 3.578 ms at batch 128. Accounted batch-128 memory was 775.78 versus 261.77 MiB. Sparse computation did not remove stored weights or dispatcher allocations. S008 S018

  7. measured

    Algebra checks gave routing-score rank four and 2.66e-15 reconstruction error. A deliberately cubic 64-matrix bank compressed 16x with near-zero error, while independent random matrices incurred 0.968 relative error. The 16x result is bank-only and depends on constructed structure. S010 S009

Limits and open questions

  • Digit results use one fixed small split; scaling uses two seeds and one image domain. Synthetic known-key recall is not natural-language knowledge.
  • Timing is eager float32 on the recorded RTX 5090 with device-resident inputs; transfer, preprocessing, startup and loading are excluded.
  • The 68.1M execution sweep is untrained. Algebraic constructions do not establish real-world model compression or language-model quality.
  • Additional sampling steps change the implemented model; reversing a drawn route does not invert computation.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: retain matched-accuracy compact residual and ordinary routing controls when evaluating a new geometric architecture.
  • Recommendation: improve dispatch before drawing broader throughput conclusions, then test additional tasks and seeds.

Bezier-Routing

Bezier-Routing

Test whether intermediate neural activations can redirect later Bezier routes, groups, and individual neurons.

Saved status Completed mechanism study; feedback works, general quality gain unestablished.
Work completed or attempted
  • Implemented individual-neuron, group, and hierarchical routing with matched entry-state controls, straight routes, and direct selection.
  • Completed 24 training runs on the reused digits split; saved checkpoints, prediction traces, intervention paths, and geometry/reload verification.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    Perturbing two activation components after hop 1 changed 44.80% of later neuron-selection sets and 16.35% of later group sets in the feedback hierarchy; the frozen hierarchy changed neither. Feedback is causally active. S019 S021

  2. measured

    Digit accuracy with versus without feedback was 95.37% versus 94.54% for individual neurons, 96.85% versus 95.93% for groups, and 94.35% versus 95.19% for the hierarchy. All paired-image 95% intervals include zero. S019 S026

  3. measured

    The trained feedback hierarchy fell from 94.35% to 89.54% when feedback was disabled and to 50.19% when neuron updates were bypassed. Reliance on a mechanism does not establish superiority to a separately trained control. S019

  4. reported

    All 24 saved checkpoints reproduced predictions and passed intervention/geometry checks; 13 unit checks were reported passed. S022 S019

Limits and open questions

  • Only three seeds and 360 reused held-out digit images; exploratory accuracy intervals exclude training-seed uncertainty.
  • Group-only executes 96 neuron calls versus 24 for individual/hierarchical modes; no clean latency advantage is claimed.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: Test fresh tasks with matched budgets and training-seed uncertainty before extending accuracy claims.

Bezier-Explore

Bezier-Explore

Determine whether shared smooth processing fields and analytic integration can reduce storage and execution cost.

Saved status Completed 90-checkpoint study; conditional storage and analytic-compute gains demonstrated.
Work completed or attempted
  • Completed a 72-run main study and 18-run second-width recall study, covering handwriting, known-key associations, and synthetic smooth conditional transforms.
  • Implemented shared nonlinear fields, commuting linear analytic fields, straight/constant/direct gates, and residual controls.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    On handwriting, shared Bezier used 510.4 KiB versus 1475.9 KiB for the original expert model (2.89x smaller), at 83.90% versus 83.88% accuracy. Constant gates reached 83.91% with still less storage, favoring weight sharing over a curvature-specific explanation. S027

  2. measured

    For 4,096 known random associations, shared Bezier achieved 100% recall at 6.05x less storage; straight, constant, direct-gate, and residual controls also reached 100%. At 16,384 associations the narrow shared model fell to 30.29%; width 256 reached 95.81% versus 99.988% for the original width-128 model. S027

  3. measured

    On the synthetic interpolation gap, analytic Bezier had normalized MSE 0.000496 versus 0.014439 for the original and used 76.9 versus 1076.8 KiB. Analytic straight was better still at 0.000386. The analytic evaluator was 1.63-3.16x faster than the smallest tested RK4 setting meeting relative output error <=1e-5. S027

  4. measured

    Robustness did not generally improve: stronger-noise recall was 74.48% versus 90.20% for 4,096 associations; noisy handwriting accuracy was 57.30% versus 61.72%. All 90 models reportedly reproduced saved test predictions. S027

Limits and open questions

  • Two seeds; width sweep only for the larger recall task; no large language-model evaluation.
  • Analytic integration requires commuting linear operators and polynomial rates; it is not a general nonlinear solver.
  • Capacity-run latency was affected by another workload; compact fields trade capacity for storage.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: Prioritize structured shared-field tasks; run quality-matched capacity/training sweeps before claiming general knowledge compression.

Bezier-Compress

Compress pretrained SmolLM2 weights using cubic fits or curve-controlled codebooks while measuring language and QA preservation.

Saved status Completed primary and exploratory compression studies; ordinary int8 is the robust preservation result.
Work completed or attempted
  • Completed 20 primary comparisons on 135M and 1.7B SmolLM2, plus a seven-case exploratory zero-anchored follow-up.
  • Compared direct/sorted cubic fitting, quantized curve/free codebooks, uniform int4/int8, and low-rank approximation; counted complete artifact bytes and independently reconstructed 22 distinct artifacts.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    Only ordinary int8 passed all prespecified validation/test preservation thresholds at both sizes. For 1.7B, int8 used 1779.56 MiB versus 3267.52 MiB FP16 and retained 99.46% of reference-correct answers. S032 S039

  2. measured

    Primary 1.7B cubic-codebook int4 used 1011.59 MiB (3.23x reduction), but perplexity rose from 10.706 to 16.200 and QA fell from 72.95% to 63.18%. Lower weight reconstruction error did not imply better model behavior. S032 S039

  3. measured

    On a separate follow-up subset, zero anchoring improved cubic-codebook perplexity from 16.551 to 12.271 and QA from 62.30% to 70.31%. It still missed preservation thresholds against FP16 (10.720 perplexity; 74.12% QA); a free zero-anchored codebook was similarly effective. S032 S040

  4. reported

    Saved completion checks record 27 evaluation cases, 22 reconstructed artifacts, and 23 passed unit tests. The tested runtime reconstructs dense FP16 weights and does not reduce steady-state GPU parameter memory. S035 S032

Limits and open questions

  • One model family, two sizes, simple quantization baselines, no compression-aware training.
  • Follow-up is a fresh subset of the same corpora, not an independent dataset; zero anchoring and control precision changed together.
  • No compressed execution kernel or demonstrated inference-speed benefit.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: Compare against stronger activation-aware quantizers and evaluate a packed runtime separately from artifact compression.

Bezier-Granite

Bezier-Granite

Adapt a local Granite 4.2 30B checkpoint to a 32 GB GPU using six-bit packed weights and test a compact feedback-routed replacement block.

Saved status Completed six-bit deployment, single-block adaptation, and later mixed-precision RL compression; smallest validation-selected screened artifact is 16.464 GiB.
Work completed or attempted
  • Built uniform6 and Bezier6 standalone artifacts, frozen language/QA screens, streamed BF16 reference evaluation, and a packed on-GPU runtime.
  • Distilled one layer-32 MLP into a compact shared field; compared curved, straight, frozen-feedback, and fixed controls; exported the replacement independently.
  • Completed a separate offline mixed-precision RL compression study: sixteen REINFORCE updates over 64 sampled section assignments, matched random search, fixed-bit controls, frozen validation selection, and a fresh final test.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    Six-bit artifacts occupy 22.245 GiB versus 54.532 GiB reference payload. On 16,384 WikiText-2 tokens and 256 ARC-Easy questions, Bezier6 had perplexity 20.0582, QA 82.03%, and 98.58% reference-correct retention, passing the project's screens. Uniform6 also passed. S041 S042

  2. measured

    Packed Bezier6 retained 22.235 GiB persistent tensors, with 23.567 GiB peak allocation; separately reconstructed dense last-token logits matched exactly. One 32-token prefill took 0.958 seconds. S041 S042

  3. measured

    The single-block standalone replacement used 21.960 GiB, saving 291.7 MiB versus the practical compressed artifact. All four fitted controls scored 81.25% QA; frozen feedback had the lowest replacement perplexity (20.2454 versus Bezier 20.2729). No added quality gain from curvature or feedback was established. S041 S042

  4. measured

    A latent perturbation changed 25.20% of later group choices in the Bezier replacement versus 0% for frozen feedback. This demonstrates adaptive routing, not better reasoning; local reconstruction relative MSE remained about 0.8021. S041 S042

  5. measured

    The later RL mixed-precision artifact used 16.464 GiB, 25.0% below the 21.960 GiB six-bit shared parent, and passed the prespecified validation and final quality screens. On the fresh 32768-token/512-question test it scored perplexity 18.2305, QA 80.86%, and original-correct retention 95.74%. S044 S046

  6. measured

    The RL policy chose 3/4/6-bit precision by four-layer section; it did not train inference-time reasoning. Fixed four-bit (15.429 GiB) and random mixed precision (15.636 GiB) also passed final test despite failing validation. RL had lower perplexity but occupied more storage; it did not establish dominance over these alternatives. S044

  7. measured

    The mixed-precision artifact retained 16.454 GiB persistent GPU tensors and peaked at 17.790 GiB for the recorded inference/KV check. Packed versus reconstructed dense last-token logits matched exactly. Large same-artifact timing variation prevented a reliable generation-speed conclusion. S044 S045

Limits and open questions

  • Limited quality screens and one block/fitting seed; no whole-model architectural conversion or broad reliability claim.
  • Eager current-matrix reconstruction targets residency, not fused low-bit speed; KV cache and driver/display memory are additional.
  • Reference size counts original tensor payload, while compressed size includes all artifact files.
  • The RL compression follow-up uses different fresh evaluation subsets from the earlier six-bit study; their perplexity and QA values must not be pooled. Its screen is point-estimate preservation, not statistical equivalence.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: Retain uniform6 as a matched control; broaden tasks/context lengths and isolate replacement training from route geometry.

Bezier-Engine

Test CPU/GPU placements for the sphere model, including supervised learning, PPO, and GPU-produced curves evaluated on CPU.

Saved status Completed execution, training, RL, and deeper-producer pipeline studies.
Work completed or attempted
  • Implemented CPU-only, GPU-only, GPU-matrix/CPU-activation, GPU-route/CPU-neuron, and CPU-actor/GPU-learner schedules.
  • Completed 54 inference configurations, 12 classifier and 15 PPO checkpoints; added pinned-buffer concurrent curve pipelines, tree workloads, and one-/four-layer control-point producers.

Findings and evidence

    Both original splits reproduced outputs/gradients but neither beat the fastest single-device baseline across nine tested inference settings. Mean digit training was 2.24 s GPU-only versus 2.82 s CPU activations and 3.41 s CPU neurons. S050 S057

  2. measured

    CPU actor/GPU learner PPO averaged 2.96 s versus 3.43 s GPU-only (about 14% less); all 15 finite-task policies reached 100% evaluation success. This is a small synchronous RL workload. S050 S057

  3. measured

    For the idle four-layer producer with 8192 independent curves, full GPU took 1.472 ms, full-batch hybrid 1.877 ms, and best concurrent hybrid 4.030 ms. The dependent tree was 2.385, 3.066, and 15.141 ms respectively. No hybrid beat the fastest baseline in the tested grid. S050

  4. reported

    Fresh one-/four-layer comparisons passed ordered output checks across 216 configurations; 70 related regression checks were reported passed. Deep producer timings use random weights rather than learned predictive performance. S050

  • One eager FP32 desktop implementation; transfer dependencies, scheduling, and untrained stress cases limit generalization.
  • Original mixed-device inference is single-request/single-stream and not thread-safe; early pipeline pilots ran with competing GPU work.
  • CPU actor/GPU learner is synchronous PPO, not a distributed or language-model RL result.

Next steps

  • Recommendation: Focus further optimization on transfer amortization or independent workloads; benchmark any kernel/pipeline change under matched uncontended conditions.

Bezier-Layers

Test content-associated layer selection across CPU/GPU boundaries, then reverse the split to keep the expert bank in system RAM.

Saved status Completed both split directions; placement and feedback verified, speed advantage unestablished.
Work completed or attempted
  • Implemented CPU-selected layers/GPU experts and GPU-selected layers/CPU experts, catalogues, grouped dispatch, cached geometry, pinned transfers, and decision-cadence controls.
  • Completed six original digit checkpoints, cached/uncached timing studies, a trained 50.9M-parameter EMNIST model, and an untrained 269.9M execution stress model.
Findings and evidence
  1. measured

    Original digit feedback switched layers on 6.20% of predictions and improved assigned-family selection from 91.48% to 95.83%; final accuracy was 95.46% versus 95.56% for separately trained entry-only selection, without a demonstrated overall improvement. S058

  2. measured

    The 50,915,372-parameter model achieved 77.95% on 9400 EMNIST test images, identical across seven execution variants. Layer switching occurred on 28.97%; maximum logit difference was 1.14e-5. S061

  3. measured

    Reversed grouped dispatch beat matching CPU-only execution in 0/12 median settings and the fastest single-device baseline in 0/12. The 269,946,720-parameter stress model was untrained. S061

  4. measured
