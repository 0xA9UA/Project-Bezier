This follow-up was specified after viewing the primary results. It evaluates seven representations on another 1,024 ARC-Easy test questions and 128 WikiText-2 windows (65,536 scored tokens), with no question ID or token-position overlap with the primary test. It is exploratory evidence from the same datasets, not an independent corpus. Compare numbers within this table; its reference scores differ from the primary subset.

Results and paired intervals · Frozen follow-up · Fresh input provenance · Primary study · Markdown report

The two new codecs anchor one of the sixteen quantization levels at zero. Bézier controls use float32 to make the anchor numerically near zero; the free codebook has an exact float16 zero. Group size, scale precision, sample budget and twenty fitting updates are unchanged. The five original control artifacts are reused. No training or evaluation-driven fitting is performed. All storage overhead is included. The numerical anchor, cubic constraint and control precision change together, so this is not an isolated causal test of zero anchoring.

The same quality targets apply: perplexity at most 1.10× the FP16 reference, QA accuracy loss at most 2 percentage points, and at least 95% retention of reference-correct answers. All three must hold. Confidence intervals resample paired questions or text blocks; they exclude checkpoint and fitting-seed uncertainty and have no multiple-comparison correction.

This study concerns model storage. Every codec reconstructs the original dense FP16 architecture; GPU parameter memory is unchanged. A smaller cubic codebook still requires a four-bit index per weight and scales per group. The sphere-routing architecture is not part of this compressor. These tests do not establish lossless knowledge preservation, greater reliability, or behavior on much larger models.
